Andrés Carrizosa

Quantitative researcher and political scientist in Asunción, Paraguay

I turn complex problems into variables that can be measured. I work at ITTI, part of Grupo Vázquez, analyzing banking, credit card, e-commerce and food delivery data. Before that, I led research teams for organizations in Paraguay and the United States.

I hold a Ph.D. in Political Science from Rice University, and I write about parties, Congress and electoral rules in Paraguay.

What I do

Data analysis for business

I segment customers, analyze transaction data and build models in Python, R and SQL. At ITTI I work with 7 group companies, including customer segmentations for ueno and Monchis.

Surveys

I design samples and questionnaires, test messages with survey experiments, and measure customer satisfaction with NPS and CSAT.

Causal inference

Instrumental variables, difference-in-differences, regression discontinuity, matching and Bayesian models.

Parties, legislatures and elections

I study how parties and electoral rules shape politics. I wrote a book on Paraguay’s Congress and write op-eds on electoral reform.

Skewing ChaosThe Role of Political Parties in Paraguay’s Legislature

Andrés Carrizosa

Carrizosa, Andrés. 2023. Skewing Chaos: The Role of Political Parties in Paraguay’s Legislature. Springer Series in Electoral Politics. Cham: Springer.

How political parties shape lawmaking in Paraguay’s Congress, a case of legislative chaos, and how that chaos can be built into existing theories of legislatures so they explain more cases.

Carrizosa, Andrés. 2018. “Paraguay 2017: Competencia política en el Legislativo, en las calles y en las urnas”. Revista de Ciencia Política 38 (2): 335–360.

A review of Paraguay’s political year in 2017, in Spanish.

Duarte, Raúl, and Andrés Carrizosa. 2023. “Getting a seat at the (electoral) table: Partisan poll workers and electoral bias”. Working paper, APSA Preprints.

Using Paraguay’s 2018 general election, we find that partisan poll workers lower their rivals’ vote shares and modestly raise their own party’s.

Carrizosa, Andrés. 2020. “The Dimensionality of Cosponsorship Behavior in the House of Representatives”. Working paper, APSA Preprints.

Bayesian item-response models show that cosponsorship in the U.S. House is multidimensional, unlike roll-call voting.
